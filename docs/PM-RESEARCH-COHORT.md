# Broader PM research cohort — October 14, 2026

## Purpose and boundaries

This seven-event set expands the October 13 manual sprint so the next user review can test more than one narrow product-event pattern. It intentionally mixes direct product/design communities, product-adjacent domain events, explicit networking formats, restrictive audiences and prices above the user's attendance limit.

The research window runs from October 17 through November 18, 2026. Two candidates extend the range five days beyond the application's default 30-day horizon because they provide unusually clear first-party evidence for product ownership, customer experience and scheduled networking. This is a research comparison, not an ingestion-window change.

No registration, payment, host contact, database write, publication, credential read, paid API request or automated score was performed. Facts and availability were checked on October 14, 2026 and can change. `Yes`, `Maybe` and `No` below refer only to the mechanically applicable provisional price band, not to a user attendance decision or a career-fit recommendation:

- `Yes`: known USD price from $0 through $150;
- `Maybe`: known USD price above $150 through $250;
- `No`: known USD price above $250; and
- `Unclassified`: no reliable public USD price.

## Decision cards

### 1. Lenny's Newsletter October Meetup in NYC

**Why it may be useful:** The organizer says the monthly meetup is for meeting fellow product managers, making connections, exploring job opportunities and discussing the tech industry. That is a more direct product-career and networking signal than a generic startup mixer.

**Practical access:** Registration requires host approval and the Brooklyn address is hidden until approval. The organizer page does not expose the date, time or price in readable page text. [FounderCal](https://foundercal.com/cities/nyc/meetups) currently places it on October 17 at 2 PM and labels it free. A previously consulted second index was unavailable during the October 14 follow-up, so those fields should be reconfirmed before any attendance decision.

**Review prompt:** Does a product-community meetup with no formal learning agenda still clear the bar because the networking audience is unusually direct?

### 2. Double Diamond — October 2026

**Why it may be useful:** The organizer targets product builders and design leaders working with AI. The agenda includes arrivals and a happy hour from 6 to 7:15 PM plus an open hang after the talks. It remains the best control for the user's prior preference: product/design focus first, with scheduled social time.

**Practical access:** The event is curated, approval is required and the exact location is hidden. The current organizer page does not render the event date or price as readable text. A current event index lists October 22, 6–11 PM and free admission: [The Juicy Times](https://juicy-times.com/events/66565-double-diamond-october-2026). Treat that as corroboration rather than a substitute for final organizer confirmation.

**Review prompt:** Does this remain the strongest option when compared with a direct PM meetup and free domain-specific conferences?

### 3. PaymentsFn 2026

**Why it may be useful:** The first-party page explicitly targets payments product owners, engineers and technical teams. It names a Director of Product Management and an SVP of Product Strategy, promises working sessions rather than only broad talks, and schedules an on-site happy hour from 4:30 to 6:30 PM.

**Practical access:** There is no fee, but seats in each session are limited. The event is specialized around payments infrastructure and agentic commerce, so its value depends on whether fintech/product-domain depth is useful. It falls five days beyond the normal 30-day discovery horizon and should not silently change that product default.

**Review prompt:** Is a specialized product-owner room with a clear happy hour more useful than a general PM community meetup?

### 4. Genesys Xperience on Tour New York

**Why it may be useful:** The first-party agenda includes an SVP of Product Management, a session on designing AI use cases, breakfast networking, a networking break and a 90-minute happy hour. The program covers customer experience, AI and regulated-industry use cases rather than generic professional networking.

**Practical access:** Genesys calls the event complimentary for its customers and new organizations and says attendance is first come, first served. The target audience is CX leaders, and the event is vendor-led, so a free ticket does not establish audience fit. It is four days beyond the normal 30-day horizon.

**Review prompt:** Does the mix of product leadership, practical CX material and scheduled networking outweigh the vendor-conference context?

### 5. Experience Research Summit: NYC

**Why it may be useful:** The half-day program is tightly focused on how research teams use AI and influence product decisions. It explicitly schedules arrival networking, guided peer discussions and a networking lunch, making the interaction evidence stronger than a vague promise to connect.

**Practical access:** This is a closed-door, application-only event for VP, Director and Head of Research/Insights leaders, limited to 50 people. No public price is shown. Unless the user's role fits that gate, the content relevance does not translate into practical access.

**Review prompt:** Should an excellent but likely inaccessible research room be `Not for me`, or remain `Maybe` pending role-fit confirmation?

### 6. Product Circle Leaders Circle — New York

**Why it may be useful:** This is the cohort's strongest explicit product-management program: a full day on product leadership, AI, organizational design and peer problem solving. The organizer limits the room to 25 people and structures the day around discussion rather than a large conference floor.

**Practical access:** The listed price is $579 excluding VAT, which is unambiguously above the user's $250 ceiling. The application is designed for senior people who lead product managers, although the organizer says it accepts a few adjacent professionals and high-potential PMs. High relevance must remain separate from the mechanical `No` price signal.

**Review prompt:** Does the price make this an immediate `No` even if it is the most directly relevant learning room?

### 7. monday.com Elevate NYC

**Why it may be useful:** Elevate advertises hands-on learning, peer roundtables, networking events and a large cross-functional audience. Its product roadmap and Product Lab are relevant, but the published role tracks center on operations, marketing, IT, sales, PMO and leadership rather than product management as a discipline.

**Practical access:** The official pricing page lists General Admission tiers from $600 through $1,200, all above the user's ceiling, and currently marks the $1,200 last-chance tier as on sale through October 28. It is a large vendor conference. A free livestream covers only the opening keynote and provides no in-person networking substitute.

**Review prompt:** Is this an easy `No` because both audience focus and price are weaker than the smaller alternatives?

## Human review protocol

Ask for an unanchored `Yes`, `Maybe` or `No` attendance decision plus one main reason for each candidate. Then ask for an overall order, allowing ties. Show the known price band and access caveats, but do not show a generated relevance score before those decisions.

After the user response:

1. Compare the decisions with the existing version 5 explanations offline, without modifying historical assessments.
2. Record whether direct PM audience, product/design practice, scheduled interaction, domain relevance, seniority restrictions or price drove each decision.
3. Treat unknown price as unknown, not free, and explicit role restrictions as access uncertainty or ineligibility rather than a relevance penalty.
4. Propose another scoring or presentation change only if a repeated mismatch has a narrow evidence-backed cause.

No additional paid discovery run is needed to review this cohort. A separate decision would be required before registration, outreach, database ingestion, publication or implementation work.
